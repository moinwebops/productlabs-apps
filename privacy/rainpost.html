Rainpost Privacy Policy

Rainpost: SWPPP Inspections · by Product Labs

Effective 24 September 2026

  • Your projects, inspection reports, photos, signatures and rain readings stay on your phone. We never receive them.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics or tracking tools.
  • Location is used only while you use the app, when you tap “Pin here” to place your site or a stormwater control.
  • If you turn on the optional rain check, the app sends only your site’s approximate location (about 3 km) to the US National Weather Service.
  • Backups are encrypted with your own passphrase and saved where you choose.

Who we are

Rainpost is made by Product Labs (“we”). This policy explains what the Rainpost Android app does with information. Questions: productlabsbd@gmail.com.

What Rainpost keeps on your phone

Rainpost is a record-keeping app for construction stormwater inspections. It stores these records in the app’s private storage on your phone:

  • Projects: name, site address, permit number, operator, the people who sign reports, work days and hours, the site map image, and the stormwater controls with their map pins and photos.
  • Rain and snow readings, including the weather station they came from.
  • Inspection reports: date and times, inspectors’ names, titles and training, findings, photos, discharges, unsafe areas, signatures and later notes.
  • Corrective actions and routine maintenance: the problem, deadlines, photos, what was done and the signature on each log entry.
  • Your company profile for report headers, and your settings.

We do not have a server for your records and we cannot see them. They leave your phone only when you share or save them yourself (see “Sharing your records”).

Location

With your permission, Rainpost reads your phone’s location only while you are using the app and only when you tap Pin here to place your site or a control on the map. The position is saved with the project. Rainpost never uses location in the background and does not track where you go. If you deny location, you can place pins by tapping the site map instead.

Rain check (optional)

The rain check is off until you turn it on. When it is on, Rainpost looks up hourly rain reported by a nearby National Weather Service station, so it can warn you when a storm may require an inspection:

Sent toWhat is sent
National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) Your site’s position rounded to a grid of about 3 km, once to find nearby stations; then only the station’s ID and the time range to look up

Nothing else is sent: no names, addresses, photos, reports or device identifiers. As with any internet request, the National Weather Service receives your phone’s IP address; see the National Weather Service privacy notice. Rainpost saves the rain amounts it receives with your project. Your own rain gauge readings always count first. You can turn the rain check off anytime in Settings.

Rainpost does not ask for your contacts, microphone, call log or background location. Photos you add from your gallery come through Android’s photo picker, which shares only the photos you pick.

Purchases

Rainpost Pro is sold through Google Play Billing. Google handles payment details under the Google Privacy Policy; we never see your card or bank details. The app checks with Google Play on your phone whether your plan is active.

No ads, analytics or tracking

Rainpost contains no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting tools. If you allow Android to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google may give us summary crash statistics through Google Play Console; these do not include your records.

Sharing your records

You decide when records leave your phone: an inspection report PDF, the corrective action log or the site binder ZIP file. Rainpost hands the file to the app you pick (for example e-mail, messaging or Google Drive), and that app’s own privacy terms apply from then on. These files contain what you recorded, including names, signatures, photos and map pins.

Encrypted backups

Backups are files encrypted with a passphrase you choose (AES-GCM). You save them where you want, such as your own Google Drive. We cannot open your backups and cannot recover a lost passphrase.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Rainpost never deletes records on its own. Construction stormwater permits usually require you to keep inspection reports and corrective action records for years after coverage ends, so the app shows the date until which a project’s records should be kept.
  • Signed reports are locked; later changes are added as dated notes.
  • To remove everything, use Settings → Delete all data, or uninstall the app. Backup and report files you saved elsewhere stay under your control until you delete them.
  • Because we do not store your records, there is nothing on our side to delete.

Security

Records and photos are kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read. The rain check uses encrypted connections (HTTPS). Each signed report carries a fingerprint so later changes can be noticed.

Children

Rainpost is a business tool for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update this page and its effective date, and mention important changes in the app’s release notes.
