Saltlog Privacy Policy

Saltlog: Snow Service Proof · by Product Labs

Effective 24 September 2026

  • Your sites, visits, photos and GPS stamps stay on your phone. We never receive them.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics or tracking tools.
  • Location is used only while you use the app, when you start or stop a visit or pin a site.
  • To fetch the weather when a visit starts, the app sends only the site's rounded, approximate location (about 3 km) to the US National Weather Service or Environment and Climate Change Canada. Nothing else is sent, and you can turn this off.
  • Backups are encrypted with your own passphrase and saved where you choose.

Who we are

Saltlog is made by Product Labs ("we"). This policy explains what the Saltlog Android app does with information. Questions: productlabsbd@gmail.com.

What Saltlog keeps on your phone

Saltlog is a record-keeping app for snow and ice services. It stores these records in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • Sites: name, address, map pin, contract type, service triggers, instructions and hazard photos.
  • Visits: start and stop times, GPS positions at start and stop, services and materials, snow depth, notes, photos, the crew member's name and the weather reading.
  • Your company profile for report headers, and your settings.

We do not have a server for your records and we cannot see them. They leave your phone only when you share or save them yourself (see "Sharing your records").

Location

With your permission, Saltlog reads your phone's location only while you are using the app, at three moments: when you tap Start, when you tap Stop, and when you tap Pin this site here. The position is saved with the visit or site so your records show where the work was done. Saltlog never uses location in the background and does not track your route. If you deny location, visits still work without GPS stamps.

Weather lookups

Weather lookups are on by default (Settings → Fetch weather automatically), and Saltlog tells you what it sends and asks you once before the first lookup. When a visit starts (or later, when your phone is back online) Saltlog looks up the current weather for the site. It uses the site's map pin, or the visit's start GPS stamp if the site has no pin, and rounds it before anything leaves the phone: to two decimal places, on a grid whose cells are at least 3 km² (about 3 km across). The exact position never leaves your phone.

Sites inSent toWhat is sent
United StatesNational Weather Service (api.weather.gov) The site's position rounded to a grid of about 3 km, then the ID of the nearest weather station
CanadaEnvironment and Climate Change Canada (api.weather.gc.ca) An area about 100 km across around the rounded position (wider if no town is nearby)

Nothing else is sent: no names, addresses, photos, records or device identifiers. As with any internet request, these government services receive your phone's IP address and may keep it in their server logs; see the National Weather Service privacy policy and the Government of Canada privacy notice (which covers api.weather.gc.ca). Saltlog saves the reading it receives (temperature, conditions, station and times) with your visit.

You can turn weather lookups off anytime in Settings → Fetch weather automatically; then nothing is sent. With weather off, or without a connection, your visit is still saved: the visit shows that no reading is available and why, and you can type the weather in yourself.

Saltlog does not ask for your contacts, microphone, call log or background location. Photos you add from your gallery come through Android's photo picker, which shares only the photos you pick.

Purchases

Subscriptions and the season pass are sold through Google Play Billing. Google handles payment details under the Google Privacy Policy; we never see your card or bank details. The app checks with Google Play on your phone whether your plan is active.

No ads, analytics or tracking

Saltlog contains no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting tools. If you allow Android to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google may give us summary crash statistics through Google Play Console; these do not include your records.

Sharing your records

You decide when records leave your phone: a PDF report, an invoice CSV, a crew file for the owner or a site list for your crew. Saltlog hands the file to the app you pick (for example e-mail, messaging or Google Drive), and that app's own privacy terms apply from then on. Reports contain what you recorded, including GPS positions, photos and names.

Encrypted backups

Backups are files encrypted with a passphrase you choose (AES-GCM). You save them where you want, such as your own Google Drive. We cannot open your backups and cannot recover a lost passphrase.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Saltlog never deletes records on its own. It reminds you to make a backup at the end of each season.
  • A visit can be deleted in the app until it locks, 24 hours after it is finished. After that it can be marked void, and it stays in reports labelled as void.
  • To remove everything, use Settings → Delete all data, or uninstall the app. Backup files you saved elsewhere stay under your control until you delete them.
  • Because we do not store your records, there is nothing on our side to delete.

Security

Records and photos are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Weather lookups use encrypted connections (HTTPS). Each record carries a SHA-256 fingerprint so changes made after a report is shared can be noticed.

Children

Saltlog is a business tool for adults. It is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your province or state), and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update this page and its effective date, and mention important changes in the app's release notes.
